An empty leg is a charter aircraft that has to fly without passengers, usually to return to base or reach its next client. It is sold at a discount to whoever can fly on its schedule. With JETCAB, empty legs are priced up to 30% below the normal fare and announced 24 to 72 hours ahead.

For a traveler with a flexible calendar, they are the closest thing to a low cost private jet, and the most affordable way into private jet charter in Mexico. This guide is written for readers in the United States who fly into or out of Mexico. It covers where empty legs come from, which routes tend to produce them, how the discount compares with published fares, and how US customs timing fits a last-minute booking.

What is an empty leg flight?

An empty leg is a repositioning flight that would otherwise operate with an empty cabin. JETCAB's aircraft are based at Toluca International Airport (TLC), 65 km from Mexico City. When a client flies one way from Toluca to Houston, Miami or Los Angeles, the jet still has to come home. When a client needs pickup in Cancún, the aircraft has to get there first. Each of those positioning flights burns fuel and crew hours whether anyone is on board or not.

Selling the empty leg lets the operator recover part of that cost. For you, it means the same aircraft, the same crew and the same private terminal as a full-fare charter, at a lower price. You still get the whole cabin. Pricing at JETCAB is always per aircraft, never per seat, and it includes crew, fuel, catering and permits.

What is the cheapest way to fly privately?

The cheapest way to fly privately is to take an empty leg when your dates allow it, in the smallest aircraft that fits your group, with every seat you are paying for filled.

The aircraft choice matters as much as the discount. A Learjet 35 or Learjet 45 carries seven passengers and is the lowest-cost jet in the fleet. Light jet flying starts at 1,700 USD per hour, against 2,700 USD for a midsize and 5,400 USD for a long range jet. Because the price covers the full aircraft, a Learjet 35 from Toluca to Houston at 3,800 USD works out to about 543 USD per person with seven travelers on board. An empty leg on that same route would bring the figure lower still.

If your dates are fixed, a standard charter is the right tool. If they are flexible by a day or two, joining the empty leg list costs nothing and gives you a shot at the lower fare.

Which routes produce empty legs into and out of Mexico?

Empty legs appear wherever one-way trips are common, and cross-border flying produces plenty of them. A family flies from Toluca to Los Angeles for a long stay, and the jet returns south the same day. A board member flies up to New York, and the aircraft comes back to base. Those return legs head toward Mexico, which suits a US traveler going south for business or a vacation.

The reverse happens too. When an aircraft is sent north to collect a client in Houston or Miami, the outbound leg from Toluca can be the empty one. Beach routes behave the same way within Mexico, with jets positioning to and from Cancún and Los Cabos in high season. JETCAB has flown more than 2,000 flights to over 200 destinations in Mexico, the United States, Latin America and Europe, so the map of possible legs is wide. Which ones open up in a given week depends entirely on other clients' trips.

Do empty legs work for international flights into the US?

They do, with one constraint you should plan around. Every private arrival into the United States requires an eAPIS passenger notice and CBP clearance, and JETCAB plans US flights with about 24 hours for that step. A leg announced 72 hours out leaves comfortable room. A leg announced the evening before may be too tight, depending on how quickly your documents are in hand.

Travelers who hold Mexican passports need a valid passport and a B1/B2 visa. ESTA does not apply on private aircraft, so a visa waiver traveler should confirm the right document before booking; CBP explains the rules on its private flyers page. Immigration and customs are handled at the private terminal where you land. For the full picture on entry rules in both directions, read our guide on flying a private plane from the US to Mexico. Requirements for entering Mexico depend on your nationality and are published by the INM.

What are the trade-offs of flying an empty leg?

The trade-off is control. The route, date and departure window come from the original client's trip, and you fit around them. If that trip moves, the empty leg moves with it.

  • You cannot add a stop or change the destination without turning the leg into a new charter.
  • Return travel is a separate question. An empty leg south does not guarantee one going back north.
  • The notice is short, which works for owners of their own schedule and poorly for anyone who needs approvals.

The sensible approach is to treat empty legs as an opportunity on top of a plan.
